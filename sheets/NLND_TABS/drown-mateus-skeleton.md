## Global map

**Low-position chord fragments and rising double stops → high-position pentatonic question/answer → middle-position dyads and octaves → a distinctive four-note arpeggio ascent → a repeated long descent → bend-driven climax → returning double stops and a tapping exit.**

The main vocabulary is E minor pentatonic, E–G–A–B–D: 1–♭3–4–5–♭7 relative to E. F# (2) and C (♭6) extend it to the E natural minor collection. F# appears in the long descending material; C is visible in measures 111, 119 and 127.

E minor is a working interpretation supported by Em voicings, pentatonic vocabulary and landing points, not an explicit author-supplied key declaration. G major shares the pitch collection. An absent key signature does not prove C major, and a chromatic note does not automatically imply modulation.

Measure 119 outlines A–C–E–F#, describable as Am6 or F#m7b5. Those names identify the same collection, not a verified backing root. Chromatic slides, bends and bar movement add tension. Some chromatic events are structural: the sustained high Eb moving toward E in 121–122 must not be discarded as a brief passing note.

## Phrase cards

### A — 105–108, p14: chord fragments carry the position shift

The low G fragments in 105 dip by a chromatic semitone and return. The D/G fourths move toward frets 12–14 in 106. Measure 107 reaches a higher B–D dyad and bar-shaped sustain; 108 leaves substantial space before a triplet pickup.

**Reduction:** stable G fragments, fourths landmarks, sustained dyad, counted silence, pickup. Add neighboring slides later. Mnemonic: `G fragments → fourths upward → stop → triplet pickup`.

### B — 109–114, pp15–16: question/answer and a lower register

Measures 109–110 use the fret-12–14 E minor pentatonic region. G14=A is prolonged in 109; G12=G answers in 110. They are not uniform scale runs. A is scale degree 4 relative to E; duration alone does not prove it is a chord tone of the unknown accompaniment.

Measures 111–112 move from G–C through upper-voice decoration toward F#–B and F#–A dyads, then descend with chromatic connection. Think voice leading rather than assigning a new backing chord to every pair. In 113, G11=F# bends a half step toward G with bar articulation; G12 also has a quarter-step bend marking. Measure 114 returns toward the middle D-string region, with G a clear anchor.

**Reduction:** sing A then G; isolate the two voices in 111–112; preserve the approach rhythm to the middle-register landing. Mnemonic: `A question → G answer → descending dyads → middle register`.

### C — 115–118, pp16–17: dyads become octaves, then silence

Measure 115 changes A–D fourths toward B–D thirds on D/G; changing the lower voice while retaining part of the upper line is more useful than memorizing unrelated grips. Measure 116 includes E–A and F#–A on A/D.

In 117, A9 and G11 are both F#, sliding to E at A7/G9: **octaves**, not thirds or a pair of different chord roots. Bar motion extends the event. Measure 118 largely rests, with an A8=F approach near the end leading toward E; this alone is not evidence of modulation.

**Reduction:** tune the F#→E octave slide without the bar first, then restore sustain, bar movement and silence. Mnemonic: `dyad voice leading → F#–E octaves → space`.

![Measure 117 descending octaves](drown-octaves.png)

**Move the same shape two frets left: F# octaves → E octaves.** Follow G and A together, muting the intervening D string instead of memorizing four unrelated notes.

### D — 119–122, pp17–18: the distinctive arpeggio cell

Measure 119 traverses E, F#, A and C across strings into the upper register, organized as two quintuplet groups. `Am triad + 6` or `F#m7b5 collection` captures its structure better than “E minor scale run.” Neither label determines the backing chord.

Measure 120 drops the note density with bar/bend articulation and rests. Measures 121–122 sustain e11=Eb toward a half-step bend to E, then answer with B at B12. The Eb is sustained expressive tension, not a disposable grace note.

**Reduction:** find A–C–E and add F#; restore the two quintuplet groups. Separately practice `long tension → E target → silence → B`. Mnemonic: `Am + 6 ascent → sparse reply → long tension toward E`.

![Measure 119 Am6-collection ascent](drown-am6-route.png)

**Outline A–C–E and add F#, rather than running another scale.** This diagram changes the reference to A to expose `1–♭3–5–6`: E becomes blue and A red. That does not establish modulation or Am6 accompaniment. The final return to C is omitted; restore both quintuplet groups from the score.

### E — 123–126, pp18–19: learn the long line once

Measures 125–126 reuse the main pitch/fret route of 123–124. Measure 124 adds bar articulation at its end, so articulation is not completely identical.

An actual G12/B12/e12 Em triad opens each pass. The subsequent line combines E minor pentatonic vocabulary, F# and local A–Bb–A decoration on the A string, descending toward the low strings. This is **a chord launch plus scalar/melodic descent**, not one continuous Em arpeggio.

Quintuplets organize important stretches. Check each bracket's beat span rather than replacing it with mechanical sixteenths.

**Reduction:** Em launch, upper G peak, cross-string descent, low-string D destination. These are selected practice anchors, not every written note. Learn the route once and repeat with the articulation difference. Mnemonic: `open Em → peak → long descent → repeat`.

### F — 127–132, pp19–20: rebuild register, then a bend-led climax

Measure 127 uses C–D–E on the D string and moves upward. C, E and the later G form useful C-triad landmarks with D connecting them; that does not establish C major accompaniment. Measure 128 rises toward B on G, touches B–D and falls back to set up the bends.

Measures 129–131 are primarily high-position E minor pentatonic. D at B15 bending a whole step to E is a major melodic event; intervening G/E landings supply contrast. Measure 132 contains unusual bend notation, including 2½ following a 3/4 marking in the preceding measure. Confirm these against recording/footage before treating the exact performed pitch or technique as settled.

**Reduction:** compare the B15 D→E bend with the unbent E at B17, preserving duration and rests. For the extreme marking, first practice the melodic contour using target pitches; verify the technique rather than forcing an excessive bend. Mnemonic: `rise again → D to E bend → hold/reply → peak`.

### G — 133–137, pp20–21: double stops return, then a tapping exit

Measure 133 descends through E–A, D–G and B–E fourths on B/e before reconnecting to middle strings: a structural echo of the opening fourths, with a different direction and string pair. Measure 134 develops dyads on A/D and D/G with neighboring-note decoration; the string-pair handoff is the important navigation event.

Measure 135 holds B at D9 under E–F–E neighboring motion on G9–10–9, then slides toward an A–D dyad before a cross-string setup. This is not an E–G third shape. Measure 136 explicitly marks high-e tapping with `T`, then returns to the middle register. Measure 137 releases E→D on the A string as the main riff returns.

**Reduction:** descending dyads and the final E→D must work without the flourish. Add tapping contact, release and muting separately before restoring its timing. Mnemonic: `fourths downward → dyad reply → tapping flourish → E–D handoff`.

## Six memory units

1. Low G fragments with chromatic neighboring motion, 105.
2. Parallel fourths, rising in 106 and descending in 133.
3. Fret-12 Em/pentatonic map and the actual Em launches in 123/125.
4. F#→E octaves in 117, with cross-string muting.
5. Am6/F#m7b5 pitch collection in 119.
6. Repeated long descent, bend climax and double-stop return from 123 onward.

Terminology should reduce the amount to memorize by explaining recurrence, shifts and destinations. Naming every event without a usable reduction does not help.

Choose tempo by timing and intonation stability, not a universal speed threshold. “Expression later” does not mean erasing structural bend targets in the first layer. Use an intelligible practice tone throughout.

Transfer exercises, **not the original composition**: keep phrase destinations but invent a new connector; reorder the measure-119 collection without moving beat boundaries; sing the upper voice of the measure-106 fourths before restoring the lower voice.
